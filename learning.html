Academic Journey

What I Have Been Learning

A breakdown of every course I have studied at AUT, what it actually taught me and how it connects to what I am building toward.

Year 1 · Foundation
COMP500
Programming Concepts and Techniques
This was where it all started. I came in with no formal programming background and by the end I was writing, debugging and testing programs in C from scratch. The fundamentals covered here, sequence, selection, iteration and functions, are things I still rely on every time I sit down to build something. What I took from it more than anything was the habit of thinking through a problem properly before touching the keyboard. C was the best language to learn that in because it is unforgiving. You cannot just throw code at the compiler and hope it works. You have to understand what you are doing and that mindset has stuck with me ever since. Having a structure like C which declares variables, types and memory management made me a more deliberate programmer. I still think about what is happening under the hood when I write code in higher level languages.
COMP501
Computing Technology in Society
This one asked questions that had me interested in a computing degree: who does technology actually serve and what happens when it fails the communities it was built for? Coming from a Maori and Pacific background those questions were personal, not theoretical. Technology has largely been built without people like me in the room. COMP501 gave me a framework for thinking about that and it changed how I approach building things. I think about who is on the other end now in a way I did not before. In this course I was able to build my first real project which was a website exhibiting our research on the social impact of bionic integration. This gave me the opportunity to research about prosthetics and robotics, something I have a keen interest in.
COMP503
Object Oriented Programming
This is the course that made programming click at a deeper level. Moving from C into Java and thinking in objects, classes, inheritance and encapsulation changed how I model problems in my head. Every complex system is just components interacting and OOP gives you the language to design those interactions deliberately. My CourtVision project I built outside of class using Java and Spring Boot came directly from what I learned here. It was the first time I felt like I could build something real. Java was another interesting language to learn because it is verbose and strict. It forces you to be deliberate about what you are doing and that is a habit I have carried into every project since. It is somewhat similar to C in that way but it is also more expressive and allows you to build more complex systems. I still use Java for backend development and it is the language I am most comfortable with.
COMP507
IT Project Management
Most students write this one off as a soft skills paper. I did not. A solutions architect does not just design systems, they manage the delivery of them. This course introduced me to Agile, project scoping, stakeholder communication and risk management. A lot of it connected directly to Woolworths where I had been doing informal versions of all of this for years without the vocabulary to describe it. COMP507 gave me that vocabulary and made me better at explaining what I actually do. Unfortunately this was a course that was heavily group reliant and many of my peers did not take it seriously. I ended up doing the majority of the work for my group and that was frustrating but it also gave me a chance to practice leadership and communication in a real context. I learned how to manage expectations, delegate tasks and keep a project on track even when others were not pulling their weight. Those are skills that have been invaluable in both my professional and extracurricular work.
COMP508
Database System Design
Every application worth building has data sitting underneath it and this course taught me how to design that layer properly. Relational databases, SQL, entity relationship modelling and normalisation. When I set up DynamoDB for SeismicStream the decisions I made about partition keys and data structure came from principles I first encountered here. Understanding how data is organised at rest makes you a meaningfully better developer because you stop treating the database as a black box.
COMP517
Data Analysis
This course sits at the boundary between statistics and computing. It introduced me to statistical thinking: how to actually interpret data, spot patterns, handle noise and reach conclusions that the evidence supports. Everyone in tech talks about being data driven but most people have a surface level understanding of what that means. This course pushed me further and it feeds directly into my AI minor and into how I think about the data coming through SeismicStream.
MATH503
Mathematics for Computing
I got an A- here and it is one I am genuinely proud of. Discrete maths is not flashy but it is the rigour underneath almost everything in computing: logic, probability, set theory, graph theory, combinatorics. These concepts underpin algorithms, cryptography and AI systems. It is also the course that showed me I could handle the mathematical side of this field, which matters when your long term goal sits at the intersection of engineering and commercial strategy.
DIGD507
Mahitahi / Collaborative Practices
Technical curricula rarely talk directly about how to work with other people. This one did. Collaborative design, cross-disciplinary communication, giving and receiving feedback and understanding the human context behind a brief. For me it connected to Maori values around collective effort that I grew up with. The skills here are ones I use constantly whether that be at work and across the student tech communities. Being good to work with is underrated in this industry.
Year 2 Semester 1 · Systems Thinking
COMP604
Operating Systems
How computers actually manage memory, processes, CPU scheduling and file systems. Why software crashes, how servers handle multiple requests at once and why cloud infrastructure is designed the way it is. This course gave me a mental model for what is happening underneath the code I write. It directly changed how I think about serverless functions and resource management in AWS. Understanding the OS layer makes you a better cloud engineer because you stop treating infrastructure as magic.
COMP610
Data Structures and Algorithms
HashMaps, trees, graphs, sorting algorithms, recursion and time complexity. I scored top marks on my assignments which showed me what I am capable of when I engage seriously with something. What I took from this course is not just the algorithms but the habit of asking why: why is this approach faster, what happens at scale, what are the tradeoffs. That question comes up in every engineering decision I make now.
INFS603
Needs Analysis, Acquisition and Training
How organisations figure out what they actually need from a technology system before building or buying one. Requirements gathering, stakeholder analysis and system evaluation. This is the solutions architect mindset in academic form. The gap between what a client says they want and what they actually need is where the real work happens. In this course I achieved two A+ grades because this material connects directly to the commercial side of technology that I am working toward and I found it genuinely interesting.
COMP613
Combinatorics and Graph Theory
Graph theory, combinatorial reasoning and the mathematical foundations of computer science. The course covered counting techniques, permutations, combinations, graph traversal algorithms and tree structures. These concepts underpin a lot of what happens in algorithm design and network analysis and understanding them at a theoretical level gives you a different way of thinking about problems involving connections, paths and relationships between data points. This course was challenging but rewarding and it has influenced how I approach problem-solving in both my academic and personal projects.
Year 2 Semester 2 · In Progress
COMP603
Program Design and Construction
Design patterns and software architecture. The difference between code that works and code that can be read, extended and debugged by someone else six months later. That distinction matters more than most students realise early on because in professional engineering you are almost never the only person working on a system. This course is making me a more deliberate developer.
COMP602
Software Development Practice
Version control workflows, testing, CI/CD, code review and Agile in practice rather than in theory. A lot of what I have been doing informally with SeismicStream and CourtVision this course is giving me the formal structure for. It is making the gap between student projects and professional engineering smaller and that is exactly what I need right now.
COMP607
Information Security Technologies
Encryption, identity and access management, threat modelling and secure system design. There is direct overlap with my AWS Cloud Practitioner cert content, particularly around IAM policies and the shared responsibility model. Security is not something you bolt on after the fact. It is part of the architecture from the start and this course is reinforcing that in both the theory and the practical work.
INFS502
Digital Services in Information Technology
How digital services are designed, delivered and managed commercially. How a product moves from idea to market, how it is priced, how it scales and how it is supported. This is the most directly relevant course to my Digital Services minor and to the business side of technology that I want to work in. It connects the technical and commercial thinking that I am trying to bring together in my career.
