What I have been learning

A breakdown of every course I have studied at AUT, what it actually taught me and how it connects to what I am building toward.

Year 1

Foundation

COMP500

Programming Concepts and Techniques

This was where it all started. I came in with no formal programming background and by the end I was writing, debugging and testing programs in C from scratch. The fundamentals covered here, sequence, selection, iteration and functions, are things I still rely on every time I sit down to build something.

What I took from it more than anything was the habit of thinking through a problem properly before touching the keyboard. C was the best language to learn that in because it is unforgiving. You cannot just throw code at the compiler and hope it works. You have to understand what you are doing and that mindset has stuck with me ever since.

Having a language like C, where you declare variables and types and manage memory yourself, made me a more deliberate programmer. I still think about what is happening under the hood when I write code in higher level languages.

COMP501

Computing Technology in Society

This one asked the questions that got me interested in a computing degree: who does technology actually serve and what happens when it fails the communities it was built for?

Coming from a Māori and Pacific background those questions were personal, not theoretical. Technology has largely been built without people like me in the room. COMP501 gave me a framework for thinking about that and it changed how I approach building things. I think about who is on the other end now in a way I did not before.

In this course I built my first real project, a website exhibiting our research on the social impact of bionic integration. It gave me the chance to research prosthetics and robotics, something I have a keen interest in.

COMP503

Object Oriented Programming

This is the course that made programming click at a deeper level. Moving from C into Java and thinking in objects, classes, inheritance and encapsulation changed how I model problems in my head. Every complex system is just components interacting and OOP gives you the language to design those interactions deliberately.

CourtVision, which I built outside of class using Java and Spring Boot, came directly from what I learned here. It was the first time I felt like I could build something real.

Java was another interesting language to learn because it is verbose and strict. It forces you to be deliberate about what you are doing and that is a habit I have carried into every project since. It is similar to C in that way, but it is more expressive and lets you build more complex systems. I still use Java for backend development and it is the language I am most comfortable with.

COMP507

IT Project Management

Most students write this one off as a soft skills paper. I did not. A solutions architect does not just design systems, they manage the delivery of them. This course introduced me to Agile, project scoping, stakeholder communication and risk management.

A lot of it connected directly to Woolworths, where I had been doing informal versions of all of this for years without the vocabulary to describe it. COMP507 gave me that vocabulary and made me better at explaining what I actually do.

This course was heavily group based and many of my peers did not take it seriously. I ended up doing most of the work for my group. That was frustrating, but it also gave me a chance to practise leadership and communication in a real context. I learned how to manage expectations, delegate tasks and keep a project on track even when others were not pulling their weight. Those skills have been invaluable in both my professional and extracurricular work.

COMP508

Database System Design

Every application worth building has data sitting underneath it and this course taught me how to design that layer properly: relational databases, SQL, entity relationship modelling and normalisation.

When I set up DynamoDB for SeismicStream, the decisions I made about partition keys and data structure came from principles I first met here. Understanding how data is organised at rest makes you a meaningfully better developer because you stop treating the database as a black box.

COMP517

Data Analysis

This course sits at the boundary between statistics and computing. It introduced me to statistical thinking: how to actually interpret data, spot patterns, handle noise and reach conclusions the evidence supports.

Everyone in tech talks about being data driven, but most people have a surface level understanding of what that means. This course pushed me further and it feeds directly into my AI minor and into how I think about the data coming through SeismicStream.

MATH503

Mathematics for Computing

I got an A- here and it is one I am genuinely proud of. Discrete maths is not flashy, but it is the rigour underneath almost everything in computing: logic, probability, set theory, graph theory and combinatorics. These concepts underpin algorithms, cryptography and AI systems.

It is also the course that showed me I could handle the mathematical side of this field, which matters when your long-term goal sits where engineering meets commercial strategy.

DIGD507

Mahitahi: Collaborative Practices

Technical curricula rarely talk directly about how to work with other people. This one did: collaborative design, cross-disciplinary communication, giving and receiving feedback and understanding the human context behind a brief.

For me it connected to the Māori values around collective effort that I grew up with. I use these skills constantly, at work and across the student tech communities. Being good to work with is underrated in this industry.

Year 2, semester 1

Systems thinking

COMP604

Operating Systems

How computers actually manage memory, processes, CPU scheduling and file systems. Why software crashes, how servers handle multiple requests at once and why cloud infrastructure is designed the way it is.

This course gave me a mental model for what is happening underneath the code I write and it directly changed how I think about serverless functions and resource management in AWS. Understanding the OS layer makes you a better cloud engineer because you stop treating infrastructure as magic.

COMP610

Data Structures and Algorithms

HashMaps, trees, graphs, sorting algorithms, recursion and time complexity. I scored top marks on my assignments, which showed me what I am capable of when I engage seriously with something.

What I took from this course is not just the algorithms but the habit of asking why. Why is this approach faster, what happens at scale and what are the tradeoffs? That question comes up in every engineering decision I make now.

INFS603

Needs Analysis, Acquisition and Training

How organisations figure out what they actually need from a technology system before building or buying one: requirements gathering, stakeholder analysis and system evaluation.

This is the solutions architect mindset in academic form. The gap between what a client says they want and what they actually need is where the real work happens. I achieved two A+ grades in this course because the material connects directly to the commercial side of technology I am working toward and I found it genuinely interesting.

COMP613

Combinatorics and Graph Theory

Graph theory, combinatorial reasoning and the mathematical foundations of computer science. The course covered counting techniques, permutations, combinations, graph traversal algorithms and tree structures.

These concepts underpin a lot of algorithm design and network analysis. Understanding them at a theoretical level gives you a different way of thinking about problems involving connections, paths and relationships between data points. It was challenging but rewarding and it has influenced how I approach problem-solving in both my academic and personal projects.

Year 2, semester 2

In progress

COMP603

Program Design and Construction

Design patterns and software architecture. The difference between code that works and code that can be read, extended and debugged by someone else six months later.

That distinction matters more than most students realise early on, because in professional engineering you are almost never the only person working on a system. This course is making me a more deliberate developer.

COMP602

Software Development Practice

Version control workflows, testing, CI/CD, code review and Agile in practice rather than in theory.

I had been doing a lot of this informally with SeismicStream and CourtVision and this course is giving me the formal structure for it. It is making the gap between student projects and professional engineering smaller and that is exactly what I need right now.

COMP607

Information Security Technologies

Encryption, identity and access management, threat modelling and secure system design. There is direct overlap with my AWS Cloud Practitioner content, particularly around IAM policies and the shared responsibility model.

Security is not something you bolt on after the fact. It is part of the architecture from the start and this course is reinforcing that in both the theory and the practical work.

INFS502

Digital Services in Information Technology

How digital services are designed, delivered and managed commercially. How a product moves from idea to market, how it is priced, how it scales and how it is supported.

This is the course most directly relevant to my Digital Services minor and to the business side of technology I want to work in. It connects the technical and commercial thinking I am trying to bring together in my career.
